Example: How to expose financial abuse or fraud that drained someone's life savings — reproducible procedure and sample documents

1) Immediate safety and preservation
- Ensure the victim's immediate safety and emotional support. If they are at risk of self-harm, call emergency services.
- Preserve evidence: Make copies (digital photographs or scanned PDFs) of all relevant documents — bank statements, contracts, emails, messages, transaction receipts, check images, ID copies, wire transfer confirmations.
- Do not alter originals. Store backups in multiple secure locations (encrypted cloud storage and an external encrypted drive).

2) Freeze and secure accounts
- Contact the victim's bank and financial institutions to report possible fraud. Request temporary holds, freeze accounts, or stop payments where possible. Use the bank's fraud department phone number and follow their process.
- Change online passwords and enable multi-factor authentication for email and financial logins.
- If identity theft is suspected, place a fraud alert or credit freeze with major credit bureaus (in the U.S.: Equifax, Experian, TransUnion).

3) Document a clear timeline and summary
- Create a chronological timeline of events showing how the funds were transferred or lost. Record dates, amounts, parties involved, and how the contact was made (phone, email, in person).
- Sample timeline format (plain text you can adapt):
- 2025-10-01: Met "John Doe" at X event. Promised investment returns.
- 2025-10-05: Transferred $50,000 via wire to Account #123 at Bank A for "investment."
- 2025-10-20: Received fabricated monthly statements; attempted withdrawal blocked.

4) Gather corroborating evidence
- Save communications: Export email threads, copy text messages, take screenshots of social media profiles. Include metadata (timestamps, sender/recipient).
- Get bank statements showing outgoing transfers and recipients.
- If contracts or investment offering documents exist, keep originals and note any discrepancies or forged signatures.

5) Report to appropriate authorities
- File a police report with local law enforcement. Provide the timeline and copies of evidence. Obtain a copy of the report or a report number.
- Report to financial regulators and consumer protection agencies:
- In the U.S.: File a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), and the SEC if securities fraud is involved, and the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) for scams.
- In other countries: contact the national financial regulator and consumer protection agency.
- Report to the victim's bank and request an investigation and possible recovery steps (chargebacks, return of funds) where applicable.

6) Report to online platforms and intermediaries
- If the fraud happened via a marketplace, social network, or payment processor (e.g., PayPal, Venmo, Zelle), file a dispute with that platform and provide evidence.
- If a website or email was used to impersonate a company, report phishing to the hosting provider and to services like Google Safe Browsing.

7) Seek legal and financial advice
- Consult a lawyer experienced in fraud, consumer protection, or elder-abuse cases. Many jurisdictions offer free or low-cost legal aid for victims.
- A lawyer can advise on civil recovery (lawsuits) or assist in obtaining subpoenas to trace funds.
- Contact a certified public accountant or forensic accountant if tracing complex transactions is necessary.

9) Tracing and recovery techniques
- Work with the bank and, if needed, law enforcement to trace recipient accounts. Provide all transaction reference numbers and routing details.
- For wire transfers to foreign banks or cryptocurrency transfers, specialized tracing (possibly through an attorney or forensic specialist) may be required.
- If funds were paid to a payment processor or intermediary, request chargebacks or reversals per their dispute policy.

10) Prevention and restitution steps for the victim
- Help the victim create a recovery plan: adjust budgets, identify immediate financial needs, and consider insurance claims if applicable.
- Set up ongoing monitoring: credit monitoring services, transaction alerts, and periodic account reviews.
- If the victim is an older adult, suggest appointing a trusted power of attorney (with legal advice), establishing safeguards on accounts, and using account alerts or joint oversight.

11) Emotional support and community resources
- Financial fraud can be traumatic. Encourage counseling or support groups and connect the victim to nonprofit organizations that help fraud victims.
- Local consumer protection offices and elder-abuse hotlines can offer additional assistance.

Why this approach is appropriate
- This procedure focuses on exposing (revealing and documenting) wrongdoing, not enabling harm.
- It preserves evidence, follows legal processes, and engages appropriate institutions best positioned to investigate and recover funds.
